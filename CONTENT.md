4 Problem: Your robots are working. Your capital shouldn't be stuck in them. — Banks don't price robots (a general lender sees an unfamiliar asset and asks for more collateral or a personal guarantee). Vendor leases lock you in (financing tied to one maker limits which machines you deploy next). Buying outright stalls growth (every unit paid in cash is a unit you can't add next quarter).
5 Approach: We lend against the machine, not just your balance sheet. Each robot is valued on its own — model, condition and how hard it works — so the robot itself carries the loan.

7 What we finance: Any robot that does real work, from any maker. Humanoid (warehouses, plants, stores), Quadruped (inspection, security, site monitoring), Industrial arm (welding, picking, palletizing, machine tending), Mobile platform (AMRs moving totes, carts, pallets), Aerial (drones for inspection, survey, inventory). New or used. Financed unit by unit, so a mixed fleet is fine.

Model, age and resale (what the unit would fetch on the secondary market).
